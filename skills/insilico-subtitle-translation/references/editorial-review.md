# Translation and review rules

Use this reference for Insilico Medicine documents, bilingual copy, and translation review. For fixed names and term pairs, read [terminology.md](terminology.md) first.

## Authority and scope

Use terminology in this order:

1. Approved project-specific glossary or required legal wording.
2. Official Insilico Chinese materials and HKEX disclosures.
3. Chinese regulatory terminology and authoritative medical standards.
4. Published papers.
5. This skill's terminology reference.

Do not silently replace wording mandated by a source document or project glossary. If it conflicts with this reference and matters to the deliverable, flag the conflict.

This is a language and translation workflow, not medical, regulatory, legal, securities-disclosure, or IP review. Do not assert that a term, project status, or approval is current without source verification appropriate to the requested material date.

## Material-sensitive editing

- Scientific, regulatory, legal, and investor materials require the closest possible preservation of scope, timing, modality, study design, and qualifiers.
- Public-facing copy may use shorter and more natural Chinese, but must not add superlatives, outcomes, or causal claims.
- Keep titles no stronger than the body text.
- Use current, officially supplied names for legal entities, research centers, websites, UI labels, and project-specific products. Registered legal names and UI labels normally remain in their source language.

## Lock before translating

Extract and preserve exactly: company and platform names; legal entities; people and titles; project IDs and aliases; generic drug names; targets; indications; study phases and endpoints; dates; numbers and units; currencies; territories; deal conditions; quotations; and paper titles.

Check project aliases against the material's date and speaker. Historical and partner identifiers are not universally interchangeable, and punctuation in IDs is significant.

## Four consistency checks

Review every high-stakes sentence for:

- **Entity:** target, molecule, project, indication, partner, and actor remain correctly linked.
- **Stage:** discovery, preclinical work, IND, clinical phase, and marketing authorization stay distinct.
- **Evidence:** uncertainty, study design, trend language, statistical significance, and limitations remain intact.
- **Commercial:** upfront payments, milestones, royalties, option rights, territories, and ceilings remain distinct.

## High-risk meaning controls

- Do not turn `may`, `might`, `potential`, `suggest`, `indicate`, `trend`, or exploratory findings into certainty or confirmed efficacy.
- Do not equate statistical significance with clinical meaningfulness, a biomarker change with clinical benefit, or a dose trend with an established dose-response relationship.
- `AE` is an adverse event; it is not necessarily an adverse reaction. `serious` and `severe` are not interchangeable.
- An IND clearance or approval authorizes the applicable clinical work; it is not marketing approval.
- Preserve whether an amount is paid, eligible, potential, conditional, or a ceiling. `Up to` means a maximum possible amount, not cash received or recognized revenue.
- Use `potentially first-in-class` or `潜在同类首创` for prospective candidate claims when the source retains that qualification. Do not manufacture `first`, `leading`, `best`, `breakthrough`, `cure`, or absolute-safety claims.

## Chinese editing and format

- Translate complete meaning and rebuild English modifier chains into clear Chinese; do not preserve English word order at the cost of readability.
- Define a non-obvious abbreviation on first use when the surrounding document must stand alone.
- Keep names, capitalization, hyphens, underscores, ranges, and units consistent. Check `million` and `billion` conversions, currencies, and conditions word for word.
- In Chinese, do not introduce spaces merely because Chinese touches English or digits, but preserve official internal spacing in names. Keep numeric-unit spacing consistent within the document and place `%` directly after the number.
- Do not confuse hyphens, minus signs, and range dashes. Use one clinical-phase style consistently within the same Chinese document.

## Delivery review

Before delivering, verify the protected items, all numbers and units, first-use abbreviations, source qualifiers, and title/body consistency. For clinical, regulatory, commercial, or superlative claims, compare the final target-language sentence back to the source and correct any stronger claim.
